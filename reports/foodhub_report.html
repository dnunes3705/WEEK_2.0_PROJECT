Problem Statement

Business Context

New York City has a rapidly growing number of restaurants, and many students and busy professionals depend on restaurant food due to limited time to cook. Online food delivery helps customers access meals from their preferred restaurants through a single app. FoodHub is a food-aggregator platform that connects customers and restaurants, assigns delivery partners to confirmed orders, tracks pickup and drop-off, collects customer ratings, and earns revenue by charging restaurants a fixed margin per delivered order.

Project Objective

FoodHub has stored historical order data for registered customers in its online portal. The objective is to analyze this order data to understand demand patterns across restaurants and customer segments, so the business can improve the customer experience and make better operational and partner (restaurant) decisions.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

KPI 1: Customer Retention Rate
Definition: Percentage of customers who remain active (place at least one order) in a subsequent time period. Why it matters: Indicates whether FoodHub is successfully keeping customers engaged over time.
KPI 2: Repeat Order Rate
Definition: Percentage of customers who place more than one order within a defined time window. Why it matters: Captures repeat behavior and helps distinguish one-time users from loyal customers.
KPI 3: Average Order Frequency
Definition: Average number of orders per customer per defined time window (e.g., per month). Why it matters: Measures depth of engagement and helps estimate customer lifetime value potential.

Key Hypotheses

Hypothesis 1: Faster and more consistent delivery experiences lead to higher customer retention.
Rationale: Long or highly variable delivery times reduce satisfaction and discourage repeat usage.
Hypothesis 2: Higher customer ratings are associated with higher repeat ordering.
Rationale: Ratings proxy overall experience quality; satisfied customers are more likely to return.
Hypothesis 3: Customers who order from a wider variety of restaurants/cuisines are more likely to be retained.
Rationale: Broader usage increases reliance on FoodHub as a primary channel for meals.
Key limitation
This dataset has no order_datetime. Retention and time-window KPIs cannot be computed as defined, so analyses use within-dataset engagement proxies such as repeat_customer_flag and n_orders.

Key Insights

  • Weekend-heavy demand: ~71% of orders occur on weekends, suggesting staffing and partner capacity decisions should prioritize weekend performance.
  • Ratings are incomplete: ~39% of orders are unrated (“Not given”), so rating-based insights are conditional on customers choosing to rate.
  • Delivery time signal is subtle: repeat customers show slightly lower average fulfillment times, but simple models show limited explanatory power without richer context (distance, location, timestamps).
  • Variety relates strongly to order count: variety measures are mechanically linked to order volume; time-based windows are needed to test causality cleanly.

Recommendations

Operational

  • Target the slowest deliveries first: identify restaurants/cuisines with long delivery tails and reduce variance (dispatch, batching rules, partner SLAs).
  • Plan for weekend load: staffing and courier supply should reflect the strong weekend skew.

Product / Measurement

  • Increase rating capture (without bias): prompt at consistent points post-delivery and monitor rating-response rates by segment.
  • Add timestamps (order_datetime) and a clear retention window definition so KPI1–KPI3 can be measured as stated.
Data to request next
  • order_datetime (or at least order_date)
  • Pickup/dropoff timestamps (or delivery pipeline events)
  • Delivery distance / zone (borough)
  • Courier identifier and batch indicators
